Three days is enough to get a real feel for Bali, as long as you do not try to see the whole island. The mistake we see most often is a plan that jumps between Ubud, Uluwatu, and Seminyak in 72 hours. On paper it works. On the road, it means most of your trip is spent in a car.

This itinerary stays around Ubud and central Bali. It is the same shape as our [Bali Essentials](/en/tours/bali-essentials/) package, written out so you can use it however you like.

## Before you go

The flight from Singapore takes under three hours, so you can land in the morning or around lunch and still have a useful first day. Check the current entry requirements and any tourism levy for your passport before you fly, since both can change.

Plan on about an hour to ninety minutes from the airport to Ubud, and more at peak times. That drive is the reason Day 1 below is gentle.

## Day 1: Arrive and ease into Ubud

Get to your Ubud hotel, drop your bags, and eat something before you go out. Then head north to the Tegalalang rice terraces. Go as early as your arrival allows. By late morning the paths are busy and the light is flat.

In the afternoon, visit Tirta Empul, a temple built around a holy spring. You can join the purification ritual or just watch. Sarongs are provided at the entrance, and shoulders and knees should be covered.

Dinner in Ubud town, then an early night. You will be glad of it tomorrow.

## Day 2: Kintamani and the craft villages

Drive up to Kintamani for the view over the Mount Batur crater. Mornings are clearer than afternoons, when cloud often rolls in. A coffee stop on the way is worth it, and a good excuse to learn how Balinese coffee is grown.

On the way back, stop in Celuk for silverwork and Mas for wood carving. These are working villages, not stages. You can watch the craftspeople, ask how pieces are made, and decide whether to buy anything. Nobody should pressure you either way.

Keep the evening free. Ubud is good for wandering, and an unplanned evening is often the part people remember.

## Day 3: A slow morning, then home

Do not pack the last day. Have breakfast, walk around Ubud or visit the market, and leave for the airport with a comfortable margin. Allow extra time on the road, because traffic is the least predictable part of Bali.

## What we left out on purpose

There is no south coast in this plan, no beach club, and no Uluwatu sunset. All of them are good, and all of them are far from Ubud. If you want the coast too, add days instead of squeezing it in. Our [Bali Explorer](/en/tours/bali-explorer/) package does this over five days.

## Practical notes

- **Weather.** The wetter months run roughly from November to March. Rain usually comes in the afternoon, so keep your outdoor stops for the morning.
- **Footwear.** Terraces and temple steps are uneven. Bring shoes you can walk in.
- **Cash.** Many small places, parking, and donations are cash only.
- **Guide and driver.** A local guide changes how these places feel, because they know the quiet hours and the stories behind each stop. More on that in [why your Bali tour needs a local guide](/en/travel-stories/why-your-bali-tour-needs-a-local-guide/).
