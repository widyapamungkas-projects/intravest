Flights booked. Hotel chosen. A few days of activities lined up.

Then someone in the group asks, “How much should we set aside for meals?”

It’s tempting to start with whatever you normally spend on lunch. If you’re used to a quick plate of chicken rice or nasi campur during the working week, that feels like a sensible benchmark.

It’s a useful starting point. But before applying it to every meal, think about what you’d actually enjoy eating—and doing—on holiday.

## A few meals worth looking forward to

There’s nothing wrong with sticking to familiar favourites. Sometimes, after a busy day out, a simple meal is exactly what you want.

But you’ve also taken time off to enjoy yourself.

Perhaps you’d love a seafood dinner in Jimbaran, with time to sit and chat afterwards. Or lunch overlooking Kintamani before continuing the day’s sightseeing.

You don’t need to order something you’ve never heard of. A familiar dish in a setting you enjoy, shared with good company, can be a lovely part of the trip.

Those are the meals worth making room for when you plan.

## You don’t need to splurge every day

A good food budget can include very different kinds of meals.

Breakfast may already be covered by your hotel. An activity package might include lunch. You can enjoy local warungs on some days and book a restaurant you’re excited about on another.

If a beachfront seafood dinner is high on your list, put more towards that evening and keep lunch simple.

The same goes for your hotel choice. Booking a comfortable hotel doesn’t oblige you to spend heavily at restaurants. You get to decide which parts of the holiday matter most.

## Look at the extra cost across the trip

Instead of considering each upgrade in isolation, add up what your preferred meals would cost over the whole holiday.

Then compare that with your original allowance.

If you’re paying for a partner, children or parents, calculate the difference for everyone. A modest increase per person can become a meaningful amount for a family.

Does the extra spending feel comfortable? Are you looking forward to those meals enough to make room for them?

If so, you have a plan. If not, keep the one you’re most excited about and adjust the others.

## Check what the price actually covers

Two “seafood dinners” can come with very different menus.

Before comparing prices, check the dishes, serving sizes, number of people sharing, and whether rice and drinks are included. Ask for the final price after any tax and service charges.

Location deserves attention too. A restaurant that fits your sightseeing route can make the evening easier, especially after a full day of activities.

A lower menu price may be less appealing if getting there means another long journey.

**You’ve planned the places you want to see. Give yourself a few meals to look forward to as well.**
